Mohaddese Moghaddam

I’m a computer science researcher working on machine learning that holds up on messy, real-world data, through causal ML and computer vision.

My journey into computer science began with a simple realization: curiosity shouldn’t be limited by the boundaries of a single discipline.

What first drew me to computer science was its ability to connect seemingly unrelated fields. Since then, my research has taken me across computer vision, causal machine learning and healthcare applications, letting me explore different scientific questions through computational approaches.

Over time, my curiosity has grown toward the nature of intelligence itself. I’m particularly fascinated by the intersection of artificial intelligence and human cognition: how computational models can help us understand the human mind, and how insights from human learning and reasoning can inspire more capable and interpretable intelligent systems.

Published at IBAAIDS 2026

Causally informed, domain-guided machine learning for imbalanced lithofacies classification from well logs

M. Moghaddam, A. Daneshpour, B. Yousefimehr, M. Ghatee

Well-log data is imbalanced and grouped by well, so a model can learn where a rock type appears instead of what it is. I encode geological knowledge as a causal graph, use it to guide feature design, and evaluate on 10 wells the model never saw in training.

  • Causal ML
  • Class imbalance
  • Generalization
  • 1.17M samples, 98 wells

Under review at Neural Computing and Applications

Cross-condition water-filled pothole segmentation: a leakage-free benchmark and tri-attention DeepLabV3+ solution

M. Moghaddam, N. Abolhasani, A. Daneshpour, B. Yousefimehr, M. Ghatee

Reflections, shadows and weather hide a pothole’s boundaries. This study asks whether segmentation models still find them in conditions they have never seen, using tri-attention, curriculum learning and diffusion-generated training data.

  • Semantic segmentation
  • Domain shift
  • Diffusion data
  • 2,874 annotated images
